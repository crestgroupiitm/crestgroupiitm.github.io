01 Feedstock Conversion · Catalysis

Chemical Transformations in Feedstock Conversion

This thrust area focuses on the fundamental chemistry underlying the thermochemical and catalytic conversion of diverse organic feedstocks. The primary objective is to identify and understand the complex chemical transformations that occur when converting materials like lignocellulosic biomass, waste plastics, algae, coals, and municipal solid waste into valuable chemicals, fuel molecules, and bioproducts.

The Challenge

The transition to a sustainable economy requires shifting away from fossil resources toward renewable and waste-derived carbon sources. However, these alternative feedstocks are highly heterogeneous and structurally complex. Lignocellulosic biomass, for instance, consists of intertwined cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, each with distinct thermal behaviors. Understanding the precise chemical pathways during thermal breakdown and subsequent catalytic upgrading is essential for maximizing product yield and quality.

Our Approach

Our research takes a fundamental approach to deconstruct these complex processes. We investigate thermochemical conversion by analyzing the initial stages of thermal degradation to understand how primary fragments are formed. We evaluate the role of heterogeneous catalysts in directing chemical pathways, including cracking, deoxygenation, isomerization, and aromatization, to tailor the final product distribution across highly oxygenated biomass to hydrogen-rich plastics.

02 Reaction Kinetics · Pyrolysis

Reaction Pathways & Kinetics of Thermal Processes

This research area delves into the intricate reaction networks and kinetics associated with advanced thermal and catalytic conversion technologies. The focus is on unraveling the specific pathways involved in processes such as fast pyrolysis, hydropyrolysis, microwave-assisted pyrolysis, and hydrodeoxygenation.

The Challenge

Thermochemical conversion processes are characterized by hundreds of simultaneous and sequential chemical reactions occurring over very short time scales. Identifying the key intermediates, determining the rate-determining steps, and understanding how reaction conditions like temperature, pressure, heating rate, catalyst presence, and hydrogen partial pressure influence the product distribution is a monumental analytical and computational challenge.

Our Approach

We employ a combination of advanced experimental techniques and kinetic modeling to untangle these complex systems. Our work spans catalytic fast pyrolysis (investigating how catalyst acidity, pore size, and shape selectivity influence reaction pathways), catalytic hydropyrolysis (studying the synergistic effects of thermal cracking with hydrogenation), microwave-assisted pyrolysis (exploring unique volumetric heating mechanisms), and hydrodeoxygenation pathways for removing oxygen from biomass-derived intermediate streams.

03 Hydrothermal Liquefaction · Scale-up

Process Intensification & Scale-up of Hydrothermal Liquefaction

This thrust area focuses on bridging the gap between fundamental research and industrial application for Hydrothermal Liquefaction (HTL). The core objective is the process intensification and scale-up of HTL technology to efficiently generate bio-crude and valuable chemicals from wet and complex feedstocks, including municipal solid wastes, plastics, and biomass agro-residues.

The Challenge

Hydrothermal Liquefaction is a highly promising technology for converting wet biomass and waste streams into energy-dense bio-crude, as it eliminates the energy-intensive drying step required for other thermal processes. However, transitioning HTL from batch-scale laboratory experiments to continuous, pilot, and commercial scales presents significant engineering challenges, including handling high pressures and temperatures, managing complex multi-phase fluid dynamics, mitigating corrosion and reactor plugging, and optimizing heat integration.

Our Approach

Our research focuses on overcoming engineering hurdles through process intensification and systematic scale-up strategies. We investigate novel reactor designs, mixing technologies, and heating strategies to enhance heat and mass transfer within the HTL environment. A major focus is the transition from batch to continuous flow systems, evaluating the rheological properties of diverse feedstock slurries and optimizing pumping and let-down systems. We rigorously test robustness using highly variable feedstocks such as MSW and mixed plastics at larger scales.

04 Materials Ageing · Dielectrics

Thermal & Electrical Ageing of Materials

This thrust area investigates the degradation mechanisms of critical industrial materials under operational stress. We focus on understanding the thermal and electrical ageing of lubricating oils and polymer composites under simulated real-world conditions, correlating ageing markers with the materials' structural and physicochemical properties.

The Challenge

Materials deployed in industrial applications, particularly in power generation and heavy machinery, are subjected to harsh environments. Lubricating oils degrade due to high temperatures, oxidation, and shear forces, leading to loss of lubricity and potential equipment failure. Polymer composites used in electrical insulation undergo complex chemical changes when exposed to sustained electrical stress and thermal cycling. Predicting the remaining useful life of these materials requires a deep understanding of fundamental degradation pathways.

Our Approach

Our research employs a comprehensive approach to study material degradation through simulated ageing environments using specialized test rigs that expose materials to precisely controlled, accelerated thermal and electrical stress profiles. We utilize advanced analytical techniques, including spectroscopy, chromatography, and thermal analysis, to identify specific chemical ageing markers. The core of our research is correlating these chemical changes with the macroscopic performance properties of the materials for predictive lifetime assessment.

05 Environmental Remediation · Advanced Materials

Advanced Functional Materials for Environmental Decontamination

This thrust area focuses on the design, synthesis, and application of novel functional materials and catalysts for advanced environmental remediation. The primary objective is to develop efficient technologies for environmental decontamination utilizing advanced oxidation processes, specifically photocatalysis and sonophotocatalysis, as well as high-capacity adsorption.

The Challenge

The pervasive contamination of water bodies with recalcitrant organic pollutants, emerging contaminants such as pharmaceuticals and microplastics, and heavy metals poses a severe threat to human health and ecosystems. Conventional water treatment technologies often struggle to completely mineralize these complex pollutants or simply transfer them from one phase to another.

Our Approach

We develop and evaluate novel materials and hybrid processes to tackle these challenging pollutants. Our work focuses on synthesizing advanced semiconductor materials, heterojunctions, and nanocomposites with tailored band gaps and enhanced charge separation to maximize reactive oxygen species generation under visible light. We investigate synergistic sonophotocatalysis to enhance mass transfer and generate additional radicals through cavitation. In parallel, we develop high-capacity, selective, and regenerable adsorption materials for targeted contaminant removal.
