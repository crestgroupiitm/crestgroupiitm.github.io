Research

Five areas of research.

From the chemistry of a single reaction to processes ready for scale-up.

  1. 01

    Chemical transformations in feedstock conversion

    The chemistry behind converting lignocellulosic biomass, waste plastics, microalgae, coals and municipal solid waste into chemicals, fuel molecules and bioproducts.

    Wood chips, waste plastics, microalgae, coal and municipal solid waste, and the bio-oil they can become.

    Learn more
  2. 02

    Reaction pathways and kinetics

    Reaction networks and kinetics of fast pyrolysis, hydropyrolysis, microwave-assisted pyrolysis and hydrodeoxygenation.

    Cellobiose, the repeat unit of cellulose, breaks down to levoglucosan, the main primary product of cellulose fast pyrolysis.

    Learn more
  3. 03

    Scale-up of hydrothermal liquefaction

    Bio-crude and chemicals from municipal solid waste, plastics and biomass residues by continuous hydrothermal processing.

    From a bench batch autoclave to a continuous tubular reactor; bio-crude settles below the aqueous phase.

    Learn more
  4. 04

    Thermal and electrical ageing of materials

    How lubricating oils and polymer composites degrade under stress, and which markers predict their lifetime.

    Lubricating oil darkening as it oxidises, and an electrical tree grown from a needle electrode in epoxy.

    Learn more
  5. 05

    Functional materials for decontamination

    Catalysts and adsorbents for photocatalysis, sonophotocatalysis and the removal of emerging contaminants.

    Methylene blue fading as photocatalysis proceeds under near-UV light, beside the catalyst powder.

    Learn more

Latest publicationPublished 7 October 2026

Adding PET reshapes the oil from waste plastics.

Microwave-assisted pyrolysis of polyethylene terephthalate with polypropylene, polystyrene, and polyethylene for resource recovery

C. Ajay Mugundan and Ravikrishnan Vinu
Journal of Material Cycles and Waste Management, 2026

  • Microwave pyrolysis
  • Graphite susceptor
  • 600 W
  • Up to 500 °C
  • 30 W/g

Blending PET with polystyrene or polyethylene raised the oil yield, while blending it with polypropylene lowered it. PET also moved the hydrocarbons in the oil towards a moderate carbon range, about C16 to C18.
